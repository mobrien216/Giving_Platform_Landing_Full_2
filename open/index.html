1. About these terms

These Terms and Conditions (“Terms”) apply to donor advised funds opened online with The Cleveland Foundation (the “Foundation”). The Foundation is a community foundation and a tax-exempt public charity described in Sections 501(c)(3), 509(a)(1), and 170(b)(1)(A)(vi) of the Internal Revenue Code (“Code”). A donor advised fund is a fund held by the Foundation, separately identified by reference to your contributions, for which you have advisory privileges as described in Section 4966 of the Code.

Your fund is also governed by the fund agreement you sign through DocuSign (“Fund Agreement”) and by the Foundation’s policies for donor advised funds, including its gift acceptance, grantmaking, investment, and fee policies, as updated from time to time (“Policies”). If these Terms conflict with your signed Fund Agreement, the Fund Agreement controls. The Foundation may update these Terms and its Policies. We’ll post changes on our website and notify you through your donor portal or by email. Continuing to use your fund after a change means you accept it.

The Foundation does not provide legal, tax, or investment advice. Please consult your own advisors.

2. Eligibility and your information

To open a fund online, you must be an individual at least 18 years old [and a U.S. resident with a U.S. bank account or card, to confirm]. Corporations, trusts, and other organizations should contact the Philanthropy team. You agree to give accurate and complete information, keep it up to date, and allow the Foundation to verify your identity and screen names against government lists as required by law. The Foundation may decline to open a fund for any reason, in which case your gift will be returned.

3. People on your fund

Donors. You are the primary donor and a fund advisor. You may name one second donor, who is also a fund advisor with the same rights you have. Donors may give to the fund, recommend grants, recommend investments, and view all fund information. Either donor may act alone.

Fund advisors. You may name up to two additional fund advisors who are not donors. Fund advisors may recommend grants and view fund information. They may not change the fund’s investments, add or remove people, or change the legacy plan. Contributions they make to the fund are their own gifts, receipted to them.

Primary advisor. The person you name as primary advisor is the Foundation’s main contact for the fund.

Financial advisor. You may give your personal financial advisor view-only or full access. With view-only access, they can see balances, statements, and activity. With full access, they can take any action in the portal that you can, and the Foundation will treat those actions as authorized by you. A financial advisor with full access, or one you recommend to manage your fund’s assets, may be considered a donor advisor under Section 4966 of the Code and subject to the same restrictions. You may remove your financial advisor at any time.

Every person on the fund will receive their own portal login. You’re responsible for making sure everyone you add agrees to follow these Terms. Changes to the people on your fund must be requested in writing [or through the portal, to confirm].

4. Gifts to your fund

Every gift to your fund is an unconditional and irrevocable charitable contribution to the Foundation. Once received, the gift belongs to the Foundation, which has exclusive legal control over the assets in your fund. Gifts cannot be returned to you or redirected to you or anyone else.

Funds opened online require an initial gift of at least $10,000 by credit or debit card or bank transfer (ACH). After your fund opens, you may add to it in any amount at any time, including gifts of stock and other assets through our Philanthropy team. Your fund is credited with the amount the Foundation actually receives. [Treatment of card processing fees: deducted from the gift, charged to the fund, or absorbed by the Foundation, to confirm]. If a payment is reversed, returned, or disputed, the Foundation may reverse the related credit and may close the fund if it has not yet opened.

By giving, you confirm that the money is yours (or yours and your spouse’s) to give and is not subject to any claim or pledge.

5. Tax matters

The Foundation will send a written acknowledgment for each gift. Whether and how much you can deduct depends on your own tax situation. You may not take a deduction when a grant is later made from your fund, and no person on the fund may receive goods, services, or other benefits in return for a gift or grant.

6. Investing your fund

Your fund is invested in one or more of the Foundation’s investment pools, overseen by the Foundation’s Investment Committee. Unless you recommend otherwise, your fund will be invested in the Flagship Pool. You may recommend an allocation across the pools offered online, and you may ask to speak with an advisor about other options. Investment recommendations are advisory, and the Foundation may decline or change them. [How often allocations may be changed, and any minimum per pool, to confirm].

Investment returns are not guaranteed. The value of your fund will rise and fall, and you could lose money. The Foundation is not liable for investment losses or for gains missed while assets move between pools.

7. Recommending grants

Donors and fund advisors may recommend grants from the fund’s available balance [minimum grant amount, to confirm]. Recommendations are advisory only. The Foundation reviews each one and may approve, decline, or change it in its sole discretion. The Foundation also holds variance power, which lets it modify any restriction or condition on the use of fund assets if, in the judgment of its Board of Directors, the restriction becomes unnecessary, incapable of fulfillment, or inconsistent with the charitable needs of the community.

Eligible recipients generally include organizations described in Section 501(c)(3) of the Code that are public charities in good standing with the IRS, and governmental units for exclusively public purposes. Other recipients may be considered with additional review.

Grants may not:

  • Be made to individuals, or to an organization for the benefit of an individual you choose
  • Pay off a pledge or other legal obligation of any person
  • Provide more than an incidental benefit to anyone on the fund or their family or related businesses, such as tuition, event or gala tickets, auction items, membership benefits, or any portion of a gift that is partly deductible
  • Pay grants, loans, compensation, or expense reimbursements to anyone on the fund or their related parties
  • Support lobbying, political campaigns, or candidates
  • Go to private non-operating foundations or certain supporting organizations restricted by Section 4966 of the Code, or to anyone on a U.S. government sanctions list

These limits reflect Sections 4958, 4966, and 4967 of the Code, which impose excise taxes on improper distributions and benefits. Each time you recommend a grant, you confirm it follows these rules. If the Foundation learns a grant was improper, it may require that it be returned.

8. Recognition and anonymity

If you choose to keep your fund anonymous, the Foundation will not share the names of the people on your fund publicly, and only Foundation staff will see them. You may also ask that individual grants be made anonymously. The Foundation may disclose information when required by law or regulators. [Whether anonymity applies by default to grant letters and published donor lists, to confirm]

9. Fees

The Foundation charges an administrative fee on each fund, tiered by fund size and deducted from the fund periodically, as described in the Foundation’s current fee schedule [link to fee schedule]. Investment management fees for each pool are reflected in that pool’s returns.

10. Legacy plan and inactive funds

If a donor dies or can no longer act, the remaining donor and fund advisors continue to advise the fund. After the lifetimes of all donors and fund advisors, the remaining balance will be distributed according to the legacy plan on file: to the Foundation’s general charitable purposes, to the charitable organizations you named, or to support the field of interest you chose. If no plan is on file, or a named organization is no longer eligible, the balance will support the Foundation’s general charitable purposes. Changes to your legacy plan must be made directly with the Foundation; instructions in a will or other document are not binding on the Foundation.

If no grants are recommended from your fund for [period, to confirm], the Foundation will try to contact you. If it cannot, it may make grants from the fund consistent with your known charitable interests or for its general charitable purposes, as allowed by its Policies.

11. Electronic communications and signatures

You agree to receive notices, acknowledgments, statements, and agreements electronically through your donor portal or email, and to sign documents electronically, including your Fund Agreement through DocuSign. Electronic signatures have the same effect as handwritten ones. You may request paper copies by contacting the Foundation.

12. General

Account security. Keep your login private. You’re responsible for activity under your login and for telling us promptly about any unauthorized use. The Foundation is not responsible for losses from unauthorized access that is not caused by the Foundation.

Errors. Please review your statements and tell us about any suspected error within [30] days.

Privacy. The Foundation handles your information under its privacy policy [link].

Closing a fund. You may ask to close your fund by recommending final grants of its full balance. The Foundation may suspend or close a fund that is used in violation of these Terms, and direct its balance to the Foundation’s general charitable purposes.

Governing law. These Terms are governed by the laws of the State of Ohio.
